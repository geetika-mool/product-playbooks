# Retrospective Checklist Template

## 1. What Went Well
- **Purpose:** Capture successes, achievements, and positive outcomes from the sprint.
- **How to Fill:** Write specific, measurable examples (e.g., “CI/CD pipeline reduced build time by 3 hours”).
- **Tips:**
  - Focus on team collaboration, process improvements, or technical wins.
  - Avoid vague statements like “things went fine.”

---

## 2. What Didn’t Go Well
- **Purpose:** Identify challenges, blockers, or inefficiencies.
- **How to Fill:** List issues that slowed progress or caused frustration (e.g., “Unclear requirements on feature 1 caused delay”).
- **Tips:**
  - Keep it constructive — focus on processes, not individuals.
  - Be honest but professional.

---

## 3. Improvements
- **Purpose:** Suggest actionable changes for the next sprint.
- **How to Fill:** Write small, testable improvements (e.g., “Define a standard review process”).
- **Tips:**
  - Improvements should be realistic and achievable within 1–2 sprints.
  - Link improvements to problems identified above.

---

## 4. Action Items
- **Purpose:** Assign ownership and deadlines to improvements.
- **How to Fill:** Use a table to track tasks, owners, due dates, and status.

| Item | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|------|-------|----------|--------|
| (Describe the improvement task) | (Assign a team member) | (Set a realistic date) | (Pending/In Progress/Done) |

- **Tips:**
  - Keep action items visible in team tools (JIRA, Confluence, Trello).
  - Review them in the next retrospective.

---

## 5. Follow-Up
- **Purpose:** Check progress on previous retrospective actions.
- **How to Fill:** Note which past improvements were completed, partially done, or not started.
- **Tips:**
  - Celebrate completed actions.
  - Discuss why incomplete actions weren’t finished and whether they’re still relevant.
